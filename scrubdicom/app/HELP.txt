SCRUB-DICOM: HOW TO USE THIS APP

What it does
  Takes a folder (or many folders) of DICOM exports and writes a separate, pseudonymised copy in which nothing in
  the header identifies the patient, the hospital or the scanner. The header rules apply to any modality; the tool
  was built and validated on cardiac CT, and "Coronary series only" and the slice-thickness audit are CT rules.
  Ultrasound and angiography often carry names burned into the pixels: check those in the viewer and redact. The original scans are never
  touched. Every output file is checked before you share anything.

  This app never connects to the internet. It starts the same engine the command line uses, as a
  separate process, and shows you its output.

Three tabs: Home, Anonymise, Help. The Anonymise tab is one journey of five steps: Open, Scans, Remove, Save, Done.
A fourth tab, Details, appears when you ask for it from the Done step. The Dark / Light button in the header switches
the look.

HOME
  Open scans (choose a folder and start), See the demo, Viewer. "This session" shows the output folder in use, how
  many patients are done and whether the check passed.

THE STATUS STRIP
  The coloured strip under the header is the one thing to watch: Not started; Anonymising n of N; Anonymised, not
  yet safe to hand over; Verified, safe to hand over; or Check failed, do not share. An amber line below it means
  something is being kept in the output (sex, shifted dates...) and it is not fully blinded.

THE DEMO
  Home or Help > "See the demo" loads two made-up patients into the ordinary four steps. Walk through them, or
  press "Play it for me". Nothing real is involved and nothing outside the app's own folder is written.

THE ANONYMISE TAB: FIVE STEPS
  Step 1  Open your scans
    First say what you are anonymising: One patient, or Several patients. Nothing else is shown until you do.
      One patient        choose that patient's folder; on the last step you type one new ID. If the folder turns
                         out to hold more than one patient the app stops and says so: it is nearly always the
                         wrong folder. "Switch to several patients" carries on with all of them.
      Several patients   choose the folder that holds all the patients; they are numbered for you on the last step.
    Every sub-folder is searched and only headers are read; nothing in the folder is changed. The list shows each patient found, the Patient ID inside
    the scans, and whether a coronary CT series was recognised.
      One folder per patient (or per study)   each gets its own new ID. Two scans of the same patient in separate
                                              folders are two studies with an ID each.
      Several patients mixed in one folder    recognised by the Patient ID inside the scans instead of by folder.
      A folder this app wrote earlier         skipped, so an old output is never anonymised twice.
    Under Several patients, "I already have a patient list (CSV) or an ID spreadsheet" shows the list-driven methods instead: a CSV of
    source_folder, study_id; or one folder of patients plus a spreadsheet of old ID, new ID. Those keep their rarely
    needed options: drive path fix, already-analysed series, spreadsheet columns. A single patient needs no list:
    choose that patient's folder.

  Step 2  Which scans do you want?
      Coronary CT only   the thin contrast coronary reconstructions (0.8 mm or thinner, 100 images or more, cardiac
                         field of view). Scouts, calcium scores, chest recons, X-rays and reports are left out.
      Everything         every series is anonymised. Types that often carry burned-in text (X-ray, ultrasound,
                         angiography, reports) are still set aside in _review for a look.
      Let me choose      tick the kinds of series to keep. A "kind" is the same modality, description and slice
                         thickness across the patients, so one tick applies to the whole cohort.
      Find               type part of a name, a type or a slice thickness ("cta", "pulm", "ct 1 mm"); every word
                         must match. "Tick shown" and "Untick shown" then act on what was found, so the many
                         names one kind of scan has across hospitals are ticked together.
    The line under the list says what would be kept. "Look at the images" opens the viewer, where the series of one
    patient can be ticked by hand; that patient's ticks then win over the choice here.

  Step 3  What should be removed?
      Everything that identifies       the default, and what a blinded read needs.
      Everything, except what I tick   sex; age as a 5-year band; weight and height; dates moved by a secret
                                       per-patient number of days; scanner make and model; scanner technical
                                       details; hospital name. These are the options DICOM PS3.15 allows.
      Use a saved profile              the built-in profiles and your own; "Edit" also sets what the patient name,
                                       study description and method text become.
    Always removed, whatever is chosen: names, date of birth, hospital and NHS numbers, addresses, doctors' names,
    accession numbers, comments, private vendor tags and the original UIDs. The choice is written into every file
    and into _logs/profile.json, and the check verifies the output against it.

  Step 4  Save and go
      New IDs            numbered from the prefix you type (ANON-001, ANON-002...). Double-click one to change it,
                         or "Fill from a spreadsheet" (old ID, new ID; the old ID may be the Patient ID inside the
                         scans or the folder name). Open the same folder again with the same two destination
                         folders and every patient keeps the ID it had.
      Anonymised copies  the output folder: one folder per new ID. Only anonymised files and the verification
                         record go here; it can be handed over.
      Confidential key   the folder for the list linking each new ID to the real patient, the UID salt and every
                         log. It must be outside the output folder and never leaves you.
      Switches           Check the output when done (leave on). Skip patients already done (leave on: a stopped
                         run carries on; half-finished patients are redone). More > "Show the rarely needed
                         options" adds: keep scanner technical details, no series sub-folders.
      Anonymise          asks once, then runs. The bar shows patient n of N and the files written.
      Preview            a dry run: reads everything and reports what would be written, writes nothing.
      Stop               ends the run cleanly. The patient in progress is redone next time.
      Open viewer        the built-in DICOM viewer (below).
    If something goes wrong a card appears above the steps with one button: Resume after a drive disconnects, Open
    report after a failed check.
    While a run is going the line under the bar says which patient, how many files, and about how long is left. A
    long run ends with a desktop notification (macOS) or the bell. "Activity log" shows the engine's own output.

  Step 5  Done
    One headline: a green tick ("N patients anonymised and verified"), an amber mark (anonymised but not yet
    checked, or changed since the check) or a red cross (the check failed: do not share).
      View the scans      the anonymised output in the viewer.
      Open the folder     the output folder.
      Hand over           compares every file with the checksum list written at the check, then opens the folder.
                          Only an unchanged output is handed over.
      Certificate (PDF)   a one-page record: how many patients and files, what was removed and kept, the result,
                          the checksum list and its SHA-256, the new IDs, what the check does not cover, and a line
                          to sign. It carries no patient identifier and is written into the output's _logs folder as
                          soon as the output verifies, so it travels with it.
      Check now / again   runs the check.
      Details             opens the Details tab (below).
    Under the buttons, anything that still needs a person: files held back for a look ("Look at them" opens the
    viewer on them), confidential logs inside the output ("Move them out"), unfinished patients.
    "What was removed" shows one of your own files in plain words: name, numbers, dates, hospital, doctor, scanner,
    each with what it became. The same card on step 3 updates as you tick what to keep.
    "Open a different output folder" shows the results of an earlier run.

  The same scan again under a new name
    The confirmation says "X is an earlier copy of this scan" and offers to remove that copy from the output first,
    so only the new ID remains. Untick it to keep both. The Done screen says when the folder also holds studies
    from earlier runs; "View the scans" opens on the one just anonymised.

  Start again
    The button at the bottom of every step clears the folder, the choices, the new IDs and the two destinations and
    returns to the first question. Nothing on disk is deleted or changed; an output already written stays where it is.

  Drag and drop
    A folder dropped anywhere on the window is opened like a chosen one. Whether it is one patient or several is
    read from what it holds.

THE VIEWER  ("Look at the images" on step 2, "Open viewer" on step 4, "View the scans" and "Look at them" on step 5)
  Two views of the same window:
    Original scans     what Anonymise WILL do, computed in memory: every series with its keep/drop decision and a
                       thumbnail, the images, and the header before and after with each removed, changed or added
                       tag highlighted. Nothing is written. "Keep this series instead" records your choice next to
                       the patient list (series_picks.csv); the run reads it.
    Anonymised output  what WAS written, plus the quarantined files in _review. Draw boxes over burned-in text,
                       then "Apply and release": the boxes are painted black in the anonymised copy, the file moves
                       into the patient's folder, and the action is logged. Run the output check again afterwards.
  Tick boxes: in the "Original scans" view every series has a "Use" tick. The rule's choices start ticked. Tick
  or untick what you want, press "Anonymise only the ticked series", and the run keeps exactly those for that
  patient (other patients are unaffected). "Let the rule decide" clears it. The ticks are saved in the
  confidential folder (series_selection.csv) and steps 2 and 4 say which patients have ticks of their own.
  Viewing: mouse wheel or arrow keys scroll slices. The "Mouse drag" switch sets what a drag does: window/level,
  zoom, or pan. The Zoom slider, the + and - buttons, cmd/ctrl + wheel and 1:1 / Fit all zoom; double-click = fit;
  right-drag always pans; space = cine. kVp, mAs and CTDIvol are shown in the series list and bottom right. Presets for coronary, soft tissue,
  lung and bone windows. Axial, coronal and sagittal reformats (the series is loaded into memory once). The
  Hounsfield value under the cursor is shown top right. "Open this file in your DICOM viewer" hands the current
  file to a viewer application of your choice: "Open in viewer app" > "Choose viewer application..." once,
  then "This file" or "This series' folder" (Bee, Horos, Weasis, MicroDicom...).

THE DETAILS TAB (Done > Details)
  Series decisions
  One row per series per patient: kept, dropped, or "needs a look" (the engine used a fallback rule because
  the header lacked contrast or cardiac-phase information, or an already-analysed pick did not match). Look at
  those. Filter, search by new ID, export as CSV.

  Check report
  Re-opens every output file and fails on any private tag, real date, original UID, vendor or institution
  text, person name, or any extra word you type: consultant surnames, the hospital's ODS code, anything
  specific to the cohort, separated by commas. The linking logs' original IDs are used automatically. The
  words you type are forgotten when you close the app. Do not share output that has not passed.

  Sharing checks and logs
  "Hand over" is disabled until the check has passed and no linking file is inside the output folder; the reasons
  are listed. Pressing it re-hashes every output file against the manifest written at verification and opens the
  folder only if nothing changed. The manifest and the attestation (what was verified, by which version, with
  which profile) travel with the output. Fix them with "Check now" and "Move confidential logs out".
  The whole _logs folder is confidential, not just the LINKAGE file: the per-file and per-patient CSVs and
  the run logs record the original folder paths, and folders are usually named by hospital number.
  "Move the patient-linking logs out" moves everything except uid_salt.txt (needed so a re-run produces the
  same UIDs) and passed check reports to a folder you choose outside the output tree. Keep that folder with
  the linkage information, away from the scans. "Open quarantined files" shows _review: secondary captures,
  reports and PDFs whose headers are clean but whose pixels may carry burned-in text. Look before sharing.

ACTIONS MENU
  List patients whose kept slices are too thick      the slice rule is 0.8 mm (0.75 mm and thinner are kept).
  Remove those patients from the output so they      deletes their folders from the OUTPUT only; the next run
  are redone                                         with "Skip patients already done" redoes them. Asks first.

IF THE DRIVE DISCONNECTS
  The run stops by itself. Reconnect the drive, leave "Skip patients already done" on, click Anonymise.

IF NOTHING HAPPENS FOR MINUTES ON ONE FILE
  The log names the file. A failing drive is hanging on it. Stop, add the full path of that file to a
  skip_files.txt next to the patient list, and run again. That patient is then flagged "needs a look" because
  a slice is missing.
